# Tempura Shrimp

## Ingredients

### Tempura batter

| Ingredient | Amount | Important note |
|---|---:|---|
| Cake flour | **100 g** | **Keep refrigerated until use** |
| Water | **180 ml** | **Must be cold** |
| Large egg | **1** | **Must be cold** |
| Extra cake flour | As needed | For lightly coating the shrimp before battering |

### Shrimp

| Ingredient | Amount | Important note |
|---|---:|---|
| Shrimp | As needed | Approximately **16 to 20 count per pound** |
| Vegetable oil | Enough for about **3 cm depth** | For frying |

> **Important: keep everything cold**
>
> The **cake flour, water, and egg should all be refrigerated before making the batter**. This includes the flour.
>
> Cold ingredients slow down gluten development. Less gluten means a lighter and crispier tempura coating.

# 1. Prepare the shrimp

Proper shrimp preparation helps prevent oil splattering, removes excess moisture, and keeps the shrimp relatively straight instead of curling during frying.

## 1.1 Peel the shrimp

Remove the shell from the body while **leaving the tail attached**.

## 1.2 Remove the pointed part above the tail

Remove the small pointed shell immediately above the tail.

> **Why remove it?**
>
> Water can remain trapped inside this part of the tail. When that water enters the hot frying oil, it can cause the oil to **pop and splatter**.

## 1.3 Devein the shrimp

Make a **shallow incision along the back**.

If you see the dark vein, remove it with the knife.

If you do not see one, **do not continue cutting deeper**. Some shrimp simply do not have a visible vein there.

## 1.4 Trim the tip of the tail

Cut a small amount from the end of the tail.

> **Why trim it?**
>
> Trimming the tail produces **cleaner, more defined edges after frying**, improving the appearance of the finished shrimp.

## 1.5 Scrape the tail

Open the tail fins.

Use the tip or edge of a knife to **scrape excess water and dirt out of the tail**.

> **Why scrape the tail?**
>
> There are two reasons:
>
> 1. **Safety:** trapped water can splatter when it enters hot oil.
> 2. **Appearance:** cleaning the tail helps it develop a more attractive **red color** during frying.

## 1.6 Wash the shrimp

Wash the prepared shrimp once to remove remaining:

- vein material
- dirt
- residue from the tail preparation

## 1.7 Dry the shrimp very thoroughly

Use paper towels to remove as much surface moisture as possible.

> **Why is drying so important?**
>
> Excess moisture is an enemy of tempura.
>
> Moist ingredients make it harder to obtain a crisp coating and can contribute to soggy tempura. Water also causes more splattering when it enters hot oil.

# 2. Straighten the shrimp

Shrimp naturally curl when heated because their muscles contract.

To keep them relatively straight, weaken those muscles before frying.

## 2.1 Make shallow belly cuts

Turn the shrimp over.

Make approximately:

**5 to 6 shallow incisions**

along the belly.

Each incision should be only about:

**2 mm deep**

Do not cut deeply through the shrimp.

## 2.2 Break the contracting muscles

Place the shrimp:

**belly side down**

on the cutting board.

Press the shrimp downward against the board with your fingers.

You should hear or feel small **breaking or popping sounds** as the muscle fibers give way.

Go over the shrimp again until you no longer hear additional breaking sounds.

> **Why break the muscles?**
>
> During frying, intact shrimp muscles contract and cause the shrimp to **curl**.
>
> Breaking these muscle fibers beforehand allows the shrimp to remain much **straighter**, producing the characteristic elongated tempura-shrimp shape.

The shrimp should now look relatively straight.

# 3. Lightly coat the shrimp with flour

Put some cake flour onto a tray.

Lightly coat **only the meat of the shrimp** with cake flour.

Shake or dust off the excess.

**Do not flour the tail.**

> **Why add a dry flour coating first?**
>
> The thin layer of cake flour helps the wet tempura batter **adhere to the shrimp**.
>
> Do not leave a thick layer of flour, because excessive flour works against the light texture you want.

# 4. Make the tempura batter

Make the batter **only after the shrimp and everything else are completely prepared**.

The batter should spend as little time as possible sitting around and warming up.

## 4.1 Everything must still be cold

Take the following directly from the refrigerator:

- **100 g cake flour**
- **180 ml water**
- **1 large egg**

This includes the flour.

> **Why everything must be cold**
>
> Gluten forms when proteins in the flour interact with water.
>
> Too much gluten makes the coating heavier, chewier, and less crisp.
>
> Several measures therefore work together:
>
> - use **cold flour**
> - use **cold water**
> - use a **cold egg**
> - mix as little as possible
> - prepare the batter immediately before frying

## 4.2 Sift the flour

Sift the **100 g cold cake flour** through a strainer.

> **Why sift it?**
>
> Sifted flour incorporates into the liquid more quickly, which helps reduce the amount of mixing needed.

## 4.3 Mix the egg and water

Combine:

- **180 ml cold water**
- **1 cold large egg**

Stir until the egg and water are well combined.

## 4.4 Add the flour gradually, approximately 1/3 at a time

Add the flour gradually rather than dumping all of it in at once.

1. Add roughly **1/3 of the flour**.
2. Gently incorporate it.
3. Add the next roughly **1/3**.
4. Gently incorporate it.
5. Add the remaining flour.
6. Stop mixing as soon as the batter has come together sufficiently.

### Do not whisk it smooth

Do not vigorously mix the batter.

Instead, gently **sink or fold the flour into the liquid**.

> **Why such gentle mixing?**
>
> Mechanical mixing encourages gluten formation.
>
> A smooth, thoroughly whisked batter is therefore **undesirable for tempura**.

## 4.5 The finished batter should be lumpy

The final batter should be:

- **very watery**
- visibly **lumpy**
- not completely homogeneous
- **not smooth like pancake batter**

> **Do not try to remove the lumps**
>
> The lumps are intentional. Continuing to whisk until they disappear would develop more gluten and produce a heavier coating.

# 5. Heat the frying oil

Fill a medium-sized pot with vegetable oil to approximately:

**3 cm depth**

Heat over medium heat.

For shrimp, the target temperature is:

**170 to 180°C**

> **Why oil temperature matters**
>
> The oil needs to be hot enough for the coating to set rapidly and become crisp.
>
> Avoid simply using maximum heat. Control the temperature and adjust the heat as needed.

# 6. Fry the tempura shrimp

Fry at:

**170 to 180°C**

Total frying time:

**approximately 1 to 1.5 minutes**

When learning the technique, frying **one shrimp at a time** makes temperature control and batter application easier.

## Step-by-step

1. Hold the prepared shrimp by its tail.
2. Dip the meat completely into the cold tempura batter.
3. Gently lower it into the hot oil.
4. Position the shrimp with its **belly facing upward**.
5. Wait for the shrimp to rise toward the surface.
6. Immediately add additional batter over it as described below.
7. Fry for a total of approximately **1 to 1.5 minutes**.
8. Remove it and let excess oil drain.

# 7. Add the characteristic extra crispy coating

Once the shrimp has risen to the surface:

1. dip your fingers into the tempura batter
2. drizzle or scatter additional batter directly over and around the frying shrimp

The additional batter attaches to the shrimp and forms the irregular, crispy exterior characteristic of tempura.

> **Why do this immediately?**
>
> Shrimp cooks very quickly.
>
> Since the complete frying time is only **1 to 1.5 minutes**, the additional batter has to be applied early enough to become crisp before the shrimp itself becomes overcooked.

# 8. Keep the oil clean and temperature stable

During frying:

- remove loose pieces of batter from the oil
- avoid overcrowding the pot
- repeatedly check the oil temperature
- adjust the heat as necessary

A small sieve or skimmer works well for removing floating batter fragments.

> **Why remove loose batter?**
>
> Old batter fragments continue frying and eventually burn, which makes the frying oil progressively dirtier.

# Most important things to keep in mind while cooking

- **Prepare the shrimp completely before making the batter**, and keep the flour, water, and egg refrigerated until that moment.
- Remove the shell but **leave the tail attached**.
- Remove the **small pointed part above the tail**, because trapped water there can cause oil splattering.
- Make a **shallow cut along the back** to check for and remove the vein. If no vein is visible, do not cut deeper.
- **Trim and scrape the tail** to remove trapped water and dirt.
- After washing, **dry the shrimp extremely thoroughly** before flouring or battering.
- Make **5 to 6 shallow belly cuts, about 2 mm deep**.
- Press the shrimp **belly side down** against the board until you no longer hear or feel the muscle fibers breaking. This helps prevent curling.
- Lightly flour **only the meat**, shake off the excess, and **leave the tail unfloured**.
- **Make the batter only immediately before frying.**
- Sift the cold flour first.
- Mix the **cold egg and cold water** before adding the flour.
- Add the flour gradually, approximately **1/3 at a time**.
- **Do not whisk vigorously.** Gently sink or fold the flour into the liquid.
- **Do not mix out the lumps.** The batter should remain thin, watery, and visibly lumpy.
- Keep the batter **cold while frying** and away from the heat of the oil as much as practical.
- Use approximately **3 cm of oil** and fry at **170 to 180°C**.
- Avoid overcrowding the pot. **One shrimp at a time is easiest when learning.**
- Hold the shrimp by the tail, coat the meat in batter, lower it gently into the oil, and fry it **belly side up**.
- As soon as the shrimp rises, **immediately drizzle additional batter over it** for the irregular crispy coating.
- Fry for only **about 1 to 1.5 minutes total**, so work quickly when adding the extra batter.
- Keep checking the oil temperature during frying and **adjust the heat as necessary**.
- **Remove loose batter pieces** between batches so they do not burn.
- Serve the tempura **as soon as possible while hot and crisp**.

> **Other ingredients**
>
> Tempura can also be made with vegetables such as **kabocha squash, carrot, broccoli, shiitake mushrooms, eggplant, and shiso leaves**.
